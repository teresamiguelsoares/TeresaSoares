A Designer at the End of Noise

What remains when noise is gone? That question started A Designer at the End of Noise. We were taught that noise is the enemy, something to remove in the name of clarity. But it’s everywhere, shaping how we communicate and what we see. So we stopped trying to fight it and started studying it. This editorial project explores noise not as interference, but as a structural part of the world we design in.

The brief

This was our final-year project, made in the second semester of third year. The whole course works as one big collective project. Every team starts from the same line, “A Designer at the End of…”, and finishes it with its own word. So each project ends up being a different take on the idea of an ending, and of the future, with the designer as an active part of it.

After a lot of talking, we chose noise. We wanted to understand how it shows up in the design world and how it has changed over time. Through text and image, our publication looks at noise in different contexts, from communication theory and graphic design to digital media and technological systems. We write about overload, excess, fragmentation, instability, saturation, repetition and interference, and about how glitch, compression, data flows and broken interfaces are the way noise appears in today’s visual culture. We didn’t want to define noise from just one angle. For us, it’s something unstable that never stops changing.

The publication

We developed an A5 editorial and chose four different papers, distributed at random through the book, to add another layer of meaning to noise. The text is organised in four chapters. I was responsible for the first one, from the research to the layout in Adobe InDesign.

01

Human Noise Is the Oldest Language That Exists

An archaeology of noise. From the cave paintings of Lascaux to Kurt Schwitters’s Merz collages, noise was never the opposite of communication, it was always its beginning. Russolo, El Lissitzky and Massin complete the arc. Noise not as disturbance, but as proof that something exists.

02

The Quieter It Gets, the More Alone We Are

The argument moves to the present. When a system removes noise efficiently, it also removes the traces of others, and what is left is not clarity but loneliness. Metahaven is the central figure. More messages, fewer people: connectivity is not the same as contact.

03

To Cancel Noise Is to Cancel Presence

The most philosophical chapter. Cancelling noise is a decision about what can stay visible, audible and real. With Hito Steyerl’s poor image and the glitch, clarity does not reveal the truth, it destroys the conditions of its existence.

04

Noise Is Proof of Others

Noise as a relational and political condition. Against Shannon’s ideal of eliminating interference, Serres’s parasite, Janet Cardiff’s Forty Part Motet and Lawrence Abu Hamdan’s forensic echoes: noise does not interrupt meaning, it carries it.

The website

In the book we couldn’t work noise through sound, so we developed a website as a complement. I built the whole website in code, in Visual Studio Code. The home page is a video grid animated with Perlin noise. An archive gallery moves sideways as you scroll, with captions that appear on hover and a modal for each piece, and an About section closes it. The visual language is built around a custom font, white titles and red accents.
