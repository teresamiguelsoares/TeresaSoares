Azucrinação

2025
Role
Research, pattern, box and record sleeves
Team
Ellen Damazio, Jorgina Züblin Da Silva, Sofia Calú, Teresa Soares
Course
Communication Design IV
Skills
Brand, Packaging, Visual identity
Tools
Adobe Illustrator, Adobe Photoshop, Figma

An edition about the role of MPB as resistance during the Brazilian military dictatorship, between 1970 and 1985. A box set with two records and a sticker album, and a website with a historical timeline, all under one visual identity.

The brief

Taking the “spirit of independence” that Rick Poynor identified in the exhibition Communicate – Independent British Graphic Design since the Sixties (London, 2004), this Communication Design IV project crosses design and music. With designers such as Malcolm Garrett, Neville Brody, Peter Saville, Vaughan Oliver and Jonathan Barnbrook as references, the aim was a special celebratory edition centred on an artist, a record or a musical style between 1970 and 2010, where digital and printed media complement each other and speculate about how music is distributed and consumed.

Theme

MPB, Brazilian popular music, emerged under the repression of the military dictatorship installed in 1964, as a language of resistance and cultural reinvention. It blended bossa nova, samba, baião, jazz and rock with political and poetic lyrics. Under surveillance and censorship, artists created metaphors and sharp irony to dodge state control, and Tropicália challenged rigid aesthetics and called for freedom.

The brief also linked the project to 50 years of democracy in Portugal, and encouraged themes such as freedom, equality, justice and solidarity. Here, music is the way into that conversation.

The identity

Azucrinação joins “azucrinar”, to annoy or disturb, with “nação”, nation. For us it is art that resists and disturbs authoritarianism with sound, poetry and courage. The name is set in a heavy, rounded display type over a geometric pattern in blue, green, yellow and orange, repeated across the box, the book and the stickers.

The edition and the book

The physical edition is a box set with two records of selected MPB songs and a sticker album that tells the story of the songs and their influence. The website, built by a teammate, adds a detailed timeline of the dictatorship. The reader is led to listen with the eyes and think with the ears.

The album has the lyrics of the songs and a short text about each one, over 16 pages printed in CMYK, 26 × 26 cm closed and bound with wire stitching. The numbered corner marks on each spread are where the stickers go, a complement that illustrates the history behind the songs.

1–12

Resistance and censorship

The first chapter gathers songs written against silence. Cálice, Meu Caro Amigo, O Bêbado e a Equilibrista and Apesar de Você are set on blue.

13–27

Tropicália and exile

The second chapter follows the artists who were arrested and exiled. Ando Meio Desligado, It’s a Long Way, Construção and Alucinação are set on green.

28–43

Identity, culture and Black roots

The last chapter is about language, origin and pride. Refavela, Ilê Ayê, Alô, Alô Marciano and Língua are set on orange.

The stickers

Each page has numbered corner marks, and each mark waits for a sticker. The stickers are archive photographs of artists and events of the period, framed by the same geometric shapes, in one colour for each chapter.

The records

Two records with the songs of the playlist AzucriNação. We selected songs and artists that were directly censored, exiled or persecuted, balancing well-known names (Caetano, Chico, Gil) with silenced voices such as Taiguara and Belchior. Each sleeve carries archive photographs of the protests and lists the songs on the back.
